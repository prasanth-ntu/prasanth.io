## Mold

### Mold vs Mildew

> [!SUMMARY] Mildew is a type of mold that grows flat on moist surfaces and is considered early-stage mold. Both are fungi that thrive in warm, damp environments — but mold penetrates deeper, causes structural damage, and poses greater health risks.

![[mold-vs-mildew-comparison.png|480]]

| | **Mold** | **Mildew** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Texture** | Fuzzy, raised filaments | Flat — powdery or downy |
| **Color** | Black, white, gray, blue, green, brown, yellow | Powdery: white → yellow/black/brown; Downy: yellow → brown |
| **Growth stage** | Advanced fungal growth | Early-stage mold (per FEMA) |
| **Damage** | Structural — decomposes materials underneath | Mostly cosmetic |
| **Health risk** | Higher severity | Lower severity, but still harmful |
| **Smell** | Stronger, tangy (like rotting wood/paper) | Sweeter, musty (like damp socks) |
| **Growth behavior** | Burrows underneath surfaces — harder to eliminate | Stays on the surface |
| **Temp range** | Similar | Thrives at 62–93°F (17–34°C) |

**Common allergy-causing molds:** *Aspergillus*, *Penicillium*, *Cladosporium*, *Alternaria*, *Stachybotrys*

**Health symptoms (Mayo Clinic / FEMA):**
- Sneezing, runny/stuffy nose, itchy eyes/nose/throat, dry scaly skin
- With asthma: coughing, wheezing, chest tightness, shortness of breath
- Headaches, nasal/sinus congestion

> [!WARNING] **Black mold** (*Stachybotrys chartarum*) can cause more severe effects: nervous system impacts, memory loss, mood changes, depression, skin/eye inflammation

**Where they grow:** Anywhere warm, dark, and moist — bathrooms, window sills, shower walls, kitchen surfaces, mattresses, attic insulation. Spores enter homes on clothes and pets.

### Prevention

- **Keep surfaces dry** — especially bathroom and kitchen; leave washing machine open between uses
- **Use a dehumidifier / moisture absorber** — maintain 40–50% indoor humidity
- **Change air filters regularly** — HVAC and HEPA filters can grow mold
- **Inspect regularly** — early detection makes treatment easier
- **Keep air circulating** — open windows, use fans; stagnant air traps humidity

### Removal

- Wear **waterproof gloves and eye protection** when cleaning
- Clean with a **mixture of bleach and warm water**
- Mildew: easier to treat yourself (surface-level, cosmetic damage)
- Mold: may need professional remediation if growth is heavy
- **Call a professional if mold covers more than 10 square feet**
- EPA guide: [Mold Cleanup in Your Home](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home)

## Pest Control

### Moths (Clothes Moths)

Clothes moths (*Tineola bisselliella*) are small beige/gold moths (~6–8mm) whose **larvae** — not the adults — eat through natural fibers like wool, silk, cashmere, cotton, and fur.

**Signs of infestation:**
- Small holes in wool/silk/cashmere garments
- Silky webbing or tiny tubes on fabrics
- Small cream-colored larvae in wardrobes/drawers
- Adult moths fluttering in closets

**Prevention:**
- **Store clean clothes** — moths are attracted to sweat, body oils, and food stains
- **Use naphthalene balls / mothballs** in wardrobes and drawers
- **Cedar blocks or lavender sachets** as natural alternatives
- **Vacuum wardrobes** and drawers regularly
- **Keep humidity low** — moths prefer humid environments
- **Seal off-season clothes** in airtight bags or containers

#### Naphthalene Balls (Mothballs)

White crystalline balls that slowly sublimate (solid → gas), releasing fumes that repel and kill moths, insects, and other pests. Also help prevent mold and musty odours in enclosed spaces.

**How to use:**
- Place a few balls in wardrobes, drawers, shoe racks, and storage boxes
- Wrap in a thin cloth or paper if you want to avoid direct contact with garments
- Replace every 1–2 months as they dissolve

> [!WARNING] **Safety precautions:**
> - Keep away from children and pets — naphthalene is toxic if ingested
> - Use only in enclosed, well-ventilated spaces
> - Do not mix with food items
> - Wash hands after handling
> - Some people are sensitive to the fumes — use cedar or lavender as alternatives
